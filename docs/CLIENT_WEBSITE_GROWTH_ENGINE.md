# Client Website Growth and Modern Marketing Engine

## Product priority: core versus optional

The product's core outcome is to help a client win qualified business and improve organic search performance against relevant competitors. The mandatory core is:

1. **Client/business discovery and qualification** based on the real offer, audience, market, intent, competitors, and verified public evidence.
2. **Business-specific outreach** with tailored email and official WhatsApp Business Platform workflows where eligible, including human approval, suppression/opt-out enforcement, safe sending limits, reply handling, and measurable outcomes.
3. **Competitor-aware SEO strategy** comparing relevant competing pages and available search-performance evidence to identify the highest-value gaps and opportunities.
4. **Website crawlability and SEO improvement** through safe audits, prioritized actionable recommendations, authorized implementation workflows, and post-change verification.

Other modules—paid advertising, social publishing, video, advanced influencer programs, digital PR campaigns, reputation management, advanced CRO, retention, and specialist local/e-commerce workflows—are optional and should be enabled only when relevant to the client and approved by them. Do not let optional breadth delay the four core workflows.

## Ranking objective and honest measurement

The system must actively pursue improved organic visibility and competitive performance; an audit-only product is insufficient. It should:

- Establish a baseline for target queries/pages and available KPIs before making recommendations.
- Use current published search-engine guidance, observed public competitor evidence, and authorized Search Console/analytics data where available. Do not claim to know private ranking algorithms or use unsupported “secret ranking factor” scores.
- Compare search intent, content completeness and usefulness, information architecture, crawl/indexability, internal linking, structured data accuracy, page experience, and credible external authority evidence where observable.
- Prioritize improvements by evidence strength, expected impact, effort, confidence, business value, and risk; execute only within client-approved permissions.
- Re-crawl and measure after changes, report what improved or regressed, and update the strategy from results.
- Track rankings and visibility for an agreed query set over a defined period, while separating correlation from proven causation.

The 90–95% improvement ambition is a product goal to pursue, not a universal guarantee: rankings are relative, query-specific, time-dependent, and affected by competition and search-engine changes outside the application's control. The application must never fake results or promise a fixed uplift. Its responsibility is to do the evidence-led work, verify changes, and make performance transparent.

## Purpose and scope

The product markets client websites and businesses, not only itself. For each client, it should build an evidence-backed growth plan spanning traditional search, AI-powered search/research discovery, technical website quality, content, distribution, conversion, retention, and measurable business outcomes. The plan adapts to business type, geography, audience, goals, budget, available data, and verified capabilities.

This document is a product specification, not proof that the listed integrations or marketing functions are implemented. No rankings, AI citations, traffic, leads, or revenue are guaranteed.

## 1. Client onboarding and authorization

- Create a separate client/workspace profile with target domains, business model, target markets/languages, audience, offer, conversion goals, budget, brand voice, competitors, legal constraints, and approved channels.
- Record domain ownership/authorization status. Read-only public audits may proceed within policy; publishing, DNS/file changes, analytics access, ad spend, email sends, social posting, and third-party account actions require appropriate verified authorization.
- Support authorized verification methods where implemented (for example DNS TXT, approved HTML file/meta token, or an authenticated webmaster integration). Never claim ownership verified without evidence.
- Import only authorized analytics/search/ad data via official APIs or user-provided exports. Explain data freshness and gaps.
- Baseline current technical health, search visibility, content coverage, conversion path, and available business KPIs before proposing work.
- Separate each client's data, credentials, drafts, reports, audiences, and campaign history by tenant and enforce access on the server.

## 2. Search and AI-agent discovery for every client website

### Technical crawlability
- Crawl only public/authorized URLs with SSRF protections, bounded depth/URL count, timeouts, size limits, redirect validation, rate limits, and respect for applicable policies.
- Inspect HTTP status, redirects, canonical URLs, robots.txt, XML sitemap(s), hreflang where relevant, internal links, broken links, duplicate/near-duplicate pages, indexability directives, pagination/facets, orphan pages, crawl traps, and mobile rendering.
- Verify that important content is available as meaningful HTML; identify key content hidden behind inaccessible client-side rendering where relevant.
- Check page titles, descriptions, headings, image alt text, descriptive anchors, language, freshness, content quality, and internal linking.
- Test structured data against visible page facts. Suggest relevant Schema.org types (such as Organization, WebSite, Article, Product, LocalBusiness, BreadcrumbList, or FAQPage only when appropriate and compliant); never fabricate ratings, reviews, prices, availability, credentials, or business details.
- Inspect performance and accessibility issues that impede people or crawlers: mobile usability, image weight, layout stability, keyboard access, contrast, semantic structure, and Core Web Vitals where data exists.
- Keep authenticated/customer-only content private. Robots directives and noindex are not access controls.

### AI search and crawler readiness
- Maintain an up-to-date, evidence-based inventory of known AI/search crawler user agents and documented controls; do not assume user-agent strings prove identity.
- Report how the site's robots.txt and server controls affect recognized crawlers. Recommend allowing suitable crawlers on public pages when the client wants discovery, while protecting private endpoints and limiting abuse.
- Check whether /llms.txt exists and, if the client wants it, propose a concise, maintained index of authoritative public pages. Explain that support is not universal and it is not a ranking factor guarantee.
- Recommend a clear, consistent entity footprint: accurate organization/product identity, authorship and credentials where real, contact details, official profiles, source-backed product/service descriptions, and consistent factual claims.
- Help produce genuinely useful, original, answerable content that directly addresses audience questions and cites reliable sources where appropriate. Make content accessible in crawlable page text rather than relying only on images or inaccessible widgets.
- Check factual consistency between website copy, product feeds/catalogues, documentation, public profiles, and structured data.
- Monitor crawler access logs or authorized webmaster/search analytics where available. Separate observed requests/indexing data from estimates.
- Do not promise that any AI system will crawl, index, cite, mention, or recommend a site. Do not use cloaking, hidden text, fake entities, synthetic reviews, mass-produced near-duplicate pages, or prompt-injection tricks intended to manipulate AI systems.

## 3. Optional marketing strategy modules

These are optional add-ons, not prerequisites for the core product. The strategy engine should select only appropriate modules rather than turn every channel on for every client.

1. **Technical SEO:** crawl/indexation, information architecture, internal links, canonicalization, sitemap/robots, redirects, duplicate content, international SEO, image SEO, structured data, mobile rendering, speed and accessibility.
2. **Content and topical authority:** audience research, topic clusters, content gap analysis, briefs, expert-reviewed articles, guides, case studies with permission, templates, tools, glossary pages, video transcripts, refresh plans, and editorial calendar.
3. **AI search / answer discovery (AEO/GEO):** answer-focused page structure, direct useful explanations, consistent entities, attributable sources, expert authorship where legitimate, accessible HTML, factual structured data, and monitoring of available referral/visibility evidence. No keyword-stuffing or guaranteed AI citations.
4. **Digital PR and earned links:** newsworthy research, data-led stories, expert commentary, partner resources, journalist outreach, and relevant editorial relationships. No paid-link schemes, link farms, automated spam, or fabricated studies.
5. **Local marketing:** accurate business profiles, location/service-area pages only where real, local citations, reviews requested ethically, local schema, and location-specific performance tracking.
6. **E-commerce/product growth:** product/category copy, feed quality, accurate Product/Offer data, image discovery, faceted navigation control, reviews from genuine customers, merchandising content, and conversion funnel checks.
7. **Social and community:** platform-specific content repurposing, short-form/video planning, creator/partner collaborations, community participation, and approved scheduling through official or permitted integrations. No fake engagement or unauthorized automated comments.
8. **Email and lifecycle marketing:** consent-aware signup, segmentation, onboarding, education, retention, reactivation and newsletters; suppression, unsubscribe, deliverability and jurisdiction/provider requirements are mandatory.
9. **Partnership and influencer outreach:** relevant prospect lists with source evidence, fit reasons, deduplication, tailored drafts, disclosure/contract tracking where appropriate, human approval, and opt-out/suppression controls.
10. **Paid acquisition:** optional campaign briefs and measurement for eligible search/social/ad platforms. Ad accounts, targeting, budgets, creative publication, and spend require explicit client authorization and caps; do not imply campaign execution where no integration exists.
11. **Conversion rate optimization (CRO):** funnel analysis, landing-page clarity, form friction, value proposition, trust signals, accessible UX, A/B test hypotheses, and measurable experiments. No deceptive patterns or dark patterns.
12. **Analytics and attribution:** define events, UTMs, funnel stages, lead quality, assisted conversions and attribution assumptions. Distinguish observed data from modeled estimates; do not fabricate ROI.
13. **Reputation and trust:** monitor public brand mentions/review trends where permitted, create factual response drafts, improve support/FAQ clarity, and escalate sensitive or legal issues to humans.
14. **Video, visual and multimedia discovery:** topic planning, accessible captions/transcripts, descriptive titles, thumbnail guidance, and valid structured metadata where applicable.
15. **Retention and customer advocacy:** onboarding content, customer education, feedback collection, referral/partner programs and case studies only with permission and verifiable evidence.

## 4. Strategy selection and prioritization

- Start with business goals: qualified leads, online sales, bookings, subscriptions, local visits, brand awareness, retention, or another explicit KPI.
- Assess the site's baseline, market, audience intent, competition, resources, seasonality, channel eligibility, budget, and confidence in the evidence.
- Rank recommendations by expected impact, confidence, effort/cost, time-to-learn, dependencies, and risk. Explain assumptions and data gaps.
- Separate quick wins, foundational fixes, experiments, and longer-term investments.
- Recommend only relevant modules. For example, local SEO may be important for a local service business but irrelevant for an online-only global SaaS.
- Build a 30/60/90-day plan only as a planning framework; timing and outcomes must reflect actual scope and capacity.
- Use a feedback loop: collect authorized data → diagnose → propose hypothesis → approval → execute → measure against baseline → retain, adjust, or stop.
- Avoid optimizing vanity metrics alone. Tie activity to qualified leads, conversion, retention, revenue where measurable, and user value.

## 5. Execution permissions and workflow

Each recommendation or campaign must show:
- Goal and target audience.
- Evidence/source URLs and observation dates.
- Current baseline, confidence, and known limitations.
- Proposed action, owner, effort, cost/budget and dependencies.
- Expected measurable outcome as a hypothesis, not a guarantee.
- Risk/compliance check and required authorization.
- Approval state, execution history, outcome, and next action.

Default workflow: audit → prioritized strategy → draft/preview → quality and compliance checks → client approval → authorized execution → monitoring → report → next iteration.

No website files, DNS, CMS, analytics, advertising budgets, email identities, or social accounts may be modified without explicit scope and authorization. Start with read-only recommendations. Automated execution must be separately enabled per channel/action, bounded by budget and rate limits, logged, reversible where feasible, and equipped with a kill switch.

## 6. Content quality and trust controls

- Use verified client facts, approved brand guidance, cited research, and source timestamps.
- Mark uncertain, stale, conflicting, or inferred claims for review.
- Require human review for regulated claims, legal/medical/financial topics, pricing, offers, guarantees, comparisons, testimonials, and reputation responses.
- Do not invent case studies, client logos, reviews, certifications, statistics, awards, backlinks, or product features.
- Avoid plagiarism, mass low-value pages, keyword stuffing, cloaking, fake engagement, manipulative link schemes, misleading scarcity, and deceptive UX.
- Keep content revision history, approval identity, publication destination, and rollback/retraction capability.
- Refresh content when facts materially change; do not change last-modified timestamps without substantive updates.

## 7. Privacy, security, and compliance

- Follow docs/SAFETY_AND_COMPLIANCE.md and applicable client/provider/jurisdiction requirements.
- Respect robots policies and third-party terms; never bypass logins, paywalls, CAPTCHAs, rate limits, or access controls.
- Protect customer analytics, prospect lists, drafts, API credentials, unpublished plans, and conversion data from public crawlers and cross-tenant access.
- Use least privilege, secure credential storage, official integrations, consent/opt-out controls, data minimization, retention/deletion, and audit logs.
- Do not target people based on sensitive traits or infer/store sensitive personal characteristics.
- Paid media budgets and external sends must have explicit client authorization, quotas, and emergency stop controls.

## 8. Reporting

Every report distinguishes:
- **Observed:** data directly collected from an authorized source.
- **Calculated:** derived from observed data using documented formulas.
- **Estimated:** modeled output with assumptions and uncertainty.
- **Recommended:** proposed action not yet executed.
- **Verified completed:** action with execution evidence.
- **Blocked/unknown:** unavailable data or unverified outcome.

Report before/after values, date ranges, data source, limitations, and the next recommended experiment. Do not attribute a result to a campaign without sufficient evidence.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

- A client website cannot enter write/publish/spend/send workflows until the required authorization and account permissions are verified.
- The audit identifies technical SEO, indexability, crawler directives, sitemap, structured data, content, mobile, accessibility and performance issues with evidence and severity.
- AI crawler recommendations are specific to the client's domain and goals, distinguish verified observations from general advice, and never promise citations/rankings.
- The plan chooses relevant strategies from the modules above and explains why excluded modules are not currently recommended.
- No publish/send/spend/write action can bypass approval settings, client quotas, provider restrictions, suppression rules, or the emergency kill switch.
- Automated tests verify tenant isolation, authorization, approval gates, quota enforcement, opt-out behavior, audit logs and rollback paths.
- Analytics reports identify source, date range, definitions and uncertainty; fabricated metrics are rejected.
- Core public pages meet the project's WCAG, technical SEO, responsive design and performance release standards.
- Documentation and UI clearly distinguish implemented integrations from planned ones.

## 10. Non-goals and honest limits

The product can improve technical readiness, coordinate authorized campaigns, generate evidence-based content, and measure outcomes where data is available. It cannot force third-party crawlers to visit/index/cite a site, guarantee rankings or revenue, bypass platform rules, or replace client approval and professional judgment in high-risk areas.
